## 스크립트

We're a Swiss robotics company building robots for industrial inspections. We're sending these robots into industrial facilities to collect data. Imagine visual data for demography, measuring temperatures, acoustics. What we do is to help the human workforce to do the tasks more efficiently, more safely, and increase the productivity of these facilities. It's very difficult to collect the data with your eyes and ears, and we cannot place sensors for every problem at every location. So what the industry needs is mobile sensing machines that can walk through environments where typically only humans can go, to collect that data. They can climb stairs, walk through narrow environments, and it's fully autonomous. Controlling these robots to walk through complex environments is very difficult. Instead of telling the robots how to walk, we let the robots learn by themselves. How we do that is deep reinforcement learning and leveraging the power of simulations, specifically the NVIDIA Isaac simulators, where we have thousands of agents in parallel and they practice and share the knowledge and very quickly convert to a neural network that we can put on the physical robot. Now, on the robot itself, we're using the NVIDIA GPUs to run these networks in real time. No connectivity needed. And the robot processes all that data and allows it to move very safely and over complex obstacles. The NVIDIA Inception program is a great collaboration framework where we can closely collaborate with the NVIDIA experts to understand the technology, where it's headed, to formulate some of the challenges we have and has allowed us to closely grow together with NVIDIA to make the best out of the technology that NVIDIA provides to us. So the vision at ANYbotics is that we create a workforce of autonomous robots. We're developing the underlying technologies in the AI autonomy and robotics that allows us to build a heterogeneous fleet of different types of robots that are optimized to accomplish the task. Eventually it's going to be different types of red robots that walk through and wander and climb through the industrial facilities and ultimately get one step closer to creating a workforce of autonomous robots.
