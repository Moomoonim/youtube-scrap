## 스크립트

We are here at the Magdalena River, one of the most endangered ecosystems in Colombia. There is less than 15 percent left of its original 4,000 square kilometers. Many species only live here, so if these forests disappear, they will disappear from the world. We cannot solve a problem if we cannot measure it. We know that for biodiversity experts to work, they need data. They need to go into the field, install new camera traps, install new acoustic devices, and then come back, sometimes finding out that the camera wasn't even working, then go back to their research units and do all the analysis. We have thousands and thousands of hours of recordings that we are not able to listen to because it's too much. But if we could analyze, we could know much more about this ecosystem. SPARROW takes recordings and other measurements, processes it using AI, and then communicates back to the cloud via satellite. SPARROW is open source. Anyone, from coastal regional scientists to researchers to park rangers, can use it and improve upon it. We are really excited to be the first pilot of Project SPARROW because it will allow us to be much more efficient in monitoring our biodiversity. This is like the perfect location to put one of our SPARROW devices, and the first one that we're actually deploying. Once it's up, they just plug the battery in and position the solar panels and the satellite antenna, and we should have internet uplink and the Wi-Fi network for the cameras. It's on! Great! So now, every single day, you're getting data from this device. That changes the whole pipeline of information. It's the chance to have all the information in real time. We are literally innovating and changing the way that we can monitor biodiversity, not just in Colombia, in the world.
