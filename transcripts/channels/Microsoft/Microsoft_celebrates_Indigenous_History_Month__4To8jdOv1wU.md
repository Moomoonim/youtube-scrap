We lost a lot of our Indigenous naming, I’m Keith Hall, and my dad, funnily enough, is Keith Hall as well. I have some great uncles who are named Keith. Even though we lost our traditional names, they still sort of carry on that naming tradition. That’s a lot of Keith's at one barbecue. Yeah it is (Laughs). Hello to many. Te Reo Māori: Kia ora tātou. Hello. Vagahau Niue: Fakalofa lahi atu. My name is Ashleigh Webster Ko e higoa haaku, ko Ashleigh Webster. I hail from the village Hau au e maaga, of Hakupu Atua, in Niue. o Hakupu Atua, i Niue. I hail from Northland (in New Zealand). Te Reo Māori: Nō Te Tai Tokerau ahau. So I’ve just introduced myself in both Te Reo Māori and Niuean. My name is Ashleigh, and I come from a little place called the Hokianga and from a place called Panguru. I'm Keith. I come from the Gamilaraay Nation in Australia. My name is Teresa Otineru. I am from the beautiful island of Samoa. So I've brought a picture of some Taonga, or things, that are really important to my Whānau and my culture. There are three Kete, which are bags. Each basket represents different milestones. We build up this collection of beautiful Taonga that we wear. I've got a picture here of our family house in Nofoali’i, where my ancestors come from. Land is so special for us, from an Indigenous standpoint. We have a spiritual connection to that land. This is the picture I've brought today. I've actually generated it using Copilot. In Aboriginal culture, we have the identity of a totem. And the totem is like our connection to animals in the land. My totem is Murray cod. So we have, like, a black cockatoo, kangaroo, and some of the native birds and lizards that essentially make up the totems of my tribe. It’s my honor to be able to sit here and just share with you all about our culture, what makes us unique, and what makes us so similar as well. We have Indigenous backgrounds, but that isn't what defines us. What defines us is the future and what we're all going to be able to be a part of.
