## 스크립트

I was saved by sports and I am enjoying my life thanks to sports. That is the degree to which I am invested in sports. I am able to live with a sense of happiness in my daily life. So actually, my life has become more enriched since I fell ill. I feel even more grateful than before. I always liked sports and moving my body. I think I was very much an attention seeker. So not only in sports, but in many other things, too, I have always been the extremely competitive type. I started playing soccer seriously when I was in the second grade. I first noticed that something was wrong was when I was playing soccer. I started to feel the pain little by little in my left leg, but I was enduring the pain even during soccer. In the end, I was in so much pain that I could hardly put on my socks. So I was already in a critical condition. I went to the hospital after, and I was immediately hospitalized. I was told not to put any more weight on my left leg, and put in a wheelchair straight away. The name of the disease was osteosarcoma. It is bone cancer, children’s cancer. From that point on, I couldn’t go home. After the surgery, as I was rehabilitating, I could no longer do ordinary things. I couldn't walk without a cane. I was really shocked when that reality hit. I really had no idea what I was going to do. But miraculously, I learned about wheelchair tennis, and I was able to switch my mindset. It was my doctor who first told me about para-sports. I watched wheelchair basketball, track and field and many other sports on YouTube. But when I watched wheelchair tennis, it was what moved me most. For that reason, I started playing wheelchair tennis a few days after I got out of the hospital. It was rather fun than difficult at first. I was having so much fun that I could do it all day long. That was the only impression I had at first. Swinging the racket was fun, and moving the wheelchair, and riding the sports wheelchair were also really fun. But I felt that individual sports were better for me after I started playing tennis. I feel more responsible and I have to think about everything by myself during the game and in practice. Also I have no one to consult with. But actually, that is one of the reasons why I enjoy it so much. I think it is similar to creating your own work. I used to hang out here when I was in elementary school. We would play soccer here. The first time I grabbed a racquet and played tennis was also here. I would say that it is the place where I started. I would practice strokes from where that bench is. It wasn’t like “let’s meet at this time”, but whenever I came, there would be someone. I would be friends with even the kids I didn’t know. After my friends found out that I became ill, a few months later, they shot a video letter here. In that sense, this place is very memorable. They told me to “stay strong”. It really helped me to hold on. After graduating from junior high school, I became a professional player. Now they all go to different high schools and I go on overseas tours. So of course we met less frequently, but we always have and will be childhood friends. Most of the people I meet now, see me as Tokito Oda, the wheelchair tennis player. So interacting with the people who have known me since I was a child is really precious. They are one of the few people who know the real me, and they are very precious to me. I really feel like I had to give up on a lot of things. I think it was really hard to watch that by my side, too. Falling ill means that you can no longer do things like before. But I was enjoying my hospital stay. So I would not say that I had gone through a difficult experience and overcome it. I only see it as a turning point in my life. I actually don't think that I overcame it, nor conquered it. It was not such a big issue for me to begin with. It was just a turning point in my life. First and foremost, I want many people to know me, in the same way that I dreamed when I fell ill. Whether you have a disability or not, it is okay to have many dreams. If you say your wishes out, they will not come true. All the practice that I do now and the titles that I win in tournaments, will eventually become the process and the past. So I would like to play in a way that I can be proud of myself in the future, and that is what I am working towards right now.
