## 스크립트

I think the story of 2027, if I could leave you with one prediction, is that we'll be talking about agent-native brands. BCG's annual CMO survey tracks how marketing leaders are dealing with AI transformation. What's the big headline this year, and why does it matter? CMOs this year, they're in the driver's seat, and the quick stat that illustrates it so well is that 50% of CMOs tell us that they're making the AI investment decisions for their function. That's almost double what it was last year. So with that great power comes great responsibility. They're also telling us that CEOs and boards are ramping up expectations, not just in terms of delivering productivity gains but also measurable growth gains. So the good news, we've gone from pilot purgatory last year to actually driving the AI transformation in the marketing function, but now we got to deliver. The survey sort of shows this widening gap between the marketing leaders who are pulling ahead and those who are being left behind. What is it they're doing differently that's leading to those outcomes? Well, and that's the headline of this year. That's the story of our CMO survey this year. We've got this wide gap between illusion and reality. Almost every CMO tells us, "I'm doing an AI transformation in my function," but look under the hood, and it's mostly a shallow transformation. The third of CMOs that are doing the hard work, they're not just scaling up a few pilots here and there in content creation or the like. They're going end to end, transforming insights, to briefing, to content, to activation. They're also doing the hard work around the tech and people processes. And if you were to offer one bit of advice to a CMO for how you make the most difference, lasting impact, over the next 12 months, what would it be? Well, our survey report findings are chockfull of practical tips from CMOs, and they do center around the people and the technology. On the people front, I think some of the practical bits of advice are, number one, how do I upskill my people? CMOs can't hire this talent, it doesn't exist. So for example, a tech CMO told us, "Every Monday morning I send my team a simple video lesson. Five minutes, learn a new skill, and try it out right away. And the whole team blocks off 50 minutes in their calendar, picks up that skill, and runs with it." Every lesson builds on the last one, and the team has been massively upskilled. You also have another pharma CMO sharing how she set up this team of AI wizards. These are curious experimenters who are constantly checking out the latest new tools in a safe environment and then bringing to the tech folks the tools that are the best ones, the next ones that they want to roll out and scale up. And then these AI wizards are also the change agents for scaling up these new tools and the processes. On the tech front, CMOs are also investing in the agentic tech stack- obviously in partnership with the CIO, CTO- they're cleaning up the data layer, that's still critical. But I think in the agentic age, there's also a new bit that they have to get right, what I call the brand intelligence layer. So this is basically competing on how your best marketers do the work and codifying that on top of your data layer so that the agents can access the data in the right way. For example, with a beauty company, we set up a briefing agent. When we simply put the agent on top of the data, it was basically useless, less than 50% accurate. When we put in place this brand intelligence layer, which was understanding which data sources to look at, which ones to trust, which ones not to trust, what questions the best marketers were asking to verify and improve the quality of the briefs, all that was coded in, the draft was 85% accurate and useful. So now we could work with that. We got real productivity gains. The campaigns got better. There was less back and forth between the agency and the internal teams. So you really do have to do the hard work, and the devil's in the details on the AI transformation. And I can't wait for next year because I think the story of 2027, if I could leave you with one prediction, is that we'll be talking about agent-native brands and how, just like we had digital-native brands, brands will figure out a way to build these processes from the start and grow faster than ever. And the established brands that don't adopt these processes, they'll be left behind. So the race is on. So much more to come. Thanks, Mark. That's right. (upbeat music)
