## 스크립트

My grandfather instilled in me a great respect for nature, but he also taught me never to fear anything. I think I would indeed say that I’m not afraid of anything. In agriculture, if you want to harvest, you have to sow, and if it’s harvest time, you have to act, as the fruit will be ruined if you sleep through it. I think that this was what ignited my interest in thinking about how we can produce energy ourselves. The vision underlying the path of WUNsiedel is to grow with one’s own resources, to inspire others, and ultimately to arrive at a model of self-sufficiency; in this case mainly in terms of energy. The approach we took at that time was to create some perspective for the city as a whole. We had lost 600 jobs, unemployment was running at almost 15 percent, and the number of people living here was on a downward spiral. We resolved to fight back, invest in the attractiveness of the city, and manage it under our own steam. And so we decided to invest in infrastructure as a city, i.e., in schools, the Luisenburg Theater, flood protection. We decided to create companies that deal with infrastructure, to create a company involved in building new homes, in refurbishing existing homes. And we said we need to take SWW further in specific directions. The investments in SWW are huge, but the business models are stable. In addition, we have gained expertise in all these areas, we have been able to draw attention to Wunsiedel, and jobs have been created. That’s practically everything you want – to keep value creation in the region. Of course, it was clear that the energy sector would make up the lion’s share, that we would be concerned to move forward with renewables and also, of course, show that it works. Then I always said, “Marco, I want you to explain how to take this forward, how we can take advantage of the opportunities that we have in the municipal utilities.” An initial challenge back then was the construction of the WUN Bioenergie facility. A biomass power plant, pellet production – and then Fukushima happened and suddenly everyone said, “Wow, you did everything right in Wunsiedel.” Even larger technology companies, such as Siemens AG, for example, took notice of us and said, “What are they doing there in the Fichtel Mountains, let’s take a look. You have households, you have industry, you have a manageable size, so it would be ideal for us to help with your network or contribute ideas.” Well, and that’s how it went on. The path of WUNsiedel isn’t finished yet; we now have the 15-million-euro WUN Pellets, we’re getting a sawmill, and the crowning glory will of course be WUN H2. All this will raise Wunsiedel to another level. It´s a great boost to Wunsiedel´s image if we can say that we are a cooperation partner with Siemens. If we realize that we need special technical designs for implementation. Or to say to people, sorry, you can do this any way you want, it's not going to work. That’s important, too. We then decided to work with Siemens to create this 8-megawatt battery as a short-term storage system. Of course, it then turned out that, more than anything else, you need good control technology. I’m not an engineer, but I would say that there is still incredible potential in the control system, right up to the control of industrial production processes. I have a great deal of knowledge about renewable energy generated by the wind and photovoltaics, and if I can then perhaps manage to make industrial production discontinuous, then I no longer need to store it. It would run synchronously. And there are many other similar issues with similar solutions. Well, and the latest stage is now, of course, the WUN H2, where we’re all waiting to break ground. This is an incredibly important development for a town like Wunsiedel. If you think about it now and know what the path of WUNsiedel is all about and what is happening here, it’s actually the logical continuation of what I learned as a child. And I’m very grateful for that. If you are the mayor of a town like Wunsiedel, there are times when you need to sit down 00:05:48,118 --&gt; 00:05:44,118 and simply have a think. Ask yourself, are we still on the right track, can I take responsibility for all this, or how could it be developed further? You don’t just need your office; you also need a different environment because you suddenly think of completely different things. A central point is the chapel and the surroundings and also the path up there. I wouldn’t want to say that it’s a mystical place, but of course it is a place where you can look over at the city, look over at the mountains – with the Katharinenberg in front of you. There's also perhaps this spiritual component, the fact that there’s a chapel here. It’s a wonderful place to sit and think.
