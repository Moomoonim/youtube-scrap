## 스크립트

AI isn't just automating work. It's a multiplier for research, for development, and for innovation at scale. When we look across the next 25 years, that means tech companies can dominate the broader economy as every other sector needs to pay for their services. In our latest report, we modeled four plausible scenarios for 2050. Across all of them, AI and technology are central. But how they develop and who benefits depends on decisions being made right now. In the AI Abundance scenario, researchers get access to transparent, open-source AI models thanks to multilateral regulations. The resulting breakthroughs in energy, materials, and medicine benefit everyone. Productivity grows at 6% a year, GDP triples. However, in Digital Darwinism, AI is concentrated in the hands of a few giant firms, accelerating R&amp;D only for those who can afford it. The richest 1% capture nearly half of all global wealth, a concentration not seen since the early 1900s. In both worlds, AI is the multiplier. The question is who it multiplies for. If all companies are buyers of AI services, leaders need to understand where their tech is coming from and where they're exposed. Taking a modular approach is the strategic move, allowing you to stay adaptable as the situation changes. And this isn't only a long-term consideration. The decisions made in the next five years will impact the next 25. Explore each scenario in our full video on YouTube or read the report at BCG.com.
