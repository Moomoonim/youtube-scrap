Developing the best performing AI agent or application requires rigorous, thorough evaluations. Whether assessing accuracy, costs, latency, safety, or any other performance dimension, deploying an application into production with confidence demands a complete end-to-end understanding of how it'll behave under all possible circumstances. To test applications, we implement scoring algorithms and build data sets that incorporate or reflect real world input. But whether using real customer data or generating synthetic data on our own, it's simply impossible to predict every possible scenario and how it'll be handled by our application. Monitoring is critical when productionizing AI. The non-deterministic nature of LLMs, evaluation data sets missing corner cases, and unanticipated changes in production environments are just a few examples of challenges confronting even the most iteration hardened AI applications. To ensure the success of our applications, it's critically important that we continually supervise their inputs and outputs, not only during development, but also after deployment into production. confidently moving our AI application into production is the result of evaluation and optimization driven by rapid iteration. During the evaluation phase, we use scores to generate metrics and track progress. We measure cost and latency to ensure that the application is meeting performance requirements. And we also keep a very close eye on whether application responses might include hallucinations or toxicity. We weigh the importance of different performance dimensions and decide what tradeoffs we might be willing to make. Are we willing to pay more for improved accuracy? Are we willing to trade a more thorough, detailed answer for faster response times? But in regards to hallucinations and toxicity, there are often trade-offs we are not willing to make. Evaluations conducted during development prior to deployment should identify unacceptable behavior and provide an opportunity to address it before an application is put in front of a customer or end user. After deployment, weave guard rails act as an additional layer of protection and can be used to safeguard your application and protect users and your application from any malicious or dangerous requests or responses. But whereas guardrails act as a barrier preventing the input or output from reaching its intended target, weave monitors record performance metrics in real time, but run asynchronously and don't interfere with the progress of a response. This allows tracking quality metrics over time using LLM as a judge scores without blocking operations in production applications. Live online evaluations conducted using monitors are especially useful for triggering alerts if scores exceed some predefined threshold or detecting data or model drift over time. All of the data collected using monitors is ideal for building new evaluation data sets because as we know the need to iterate and optimize doesn't end simply because an application has been deployed into production.
