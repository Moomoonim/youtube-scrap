## 스크립트

“We do not inherit the earth from our ancestors; we borrow it from our children.” And that, in a nutshell, is what drives me. That we have to manage with the resources that the sun blesses our planet with every day – wind, heat, and the growth of biomass. We need to make a sea change and discover new pathways to making our prosperity, our well-being, our luxury goods, everything we care about, more sustainable. It’s essentially a matter of decarbonizing the world, our system, and making do with what I like to call “the superterranean energies”: sun, wind, water, biomass. Below the ground we have coal, nuclear, and oil. Protecting the environment is only possible with renewables. Only renewables are permanently cost-effective, as the sun doesn’t charge. And permanence is only possible with intelligence and tech partnerships like the one we were fortunate enough to enter into with Siemens AG. After all, knowledge is one thing, putting it into practice another. And so we feel included and encouraged on the path to achieving a renewable future, or the path of WUNsiedel. We’re trying to shape, to live the future of energy. And now it’s possible on a distributed basis – i.e., wind turbines are decentralized, photovoltaic systems even more so, on every building – it is our duty and job to establish these systems, to install them and tell everyone who’s willing to listen, as well as those who aren’t, that this system is cheaper, more efficient, and more reliable than what we have today. The path of WUNsiedel is based on the three elements of generation, distribution, and storage. The essential elements for distributed energy supply. In terms of generation, it’s already established. There are photovoltaic installations everywhere, wind turbines, too. Siemens comes into play for the intelligence, i.e., intelligent interlinking, intelligent distribution networks with MindSphere. Short and long-term storage with the lithium-ion battery storage system in 2016, with hydrogen, now just in time. With the complex question of the power plant of the future. So how can we control fluctuating energy generation systems powered by the wind and sun? How do we combine the whole thing with sales? In a word: demand-side management. 00:02:46,218 --&gt; 00:02:49,218 And here Siemens has a great deal to offer, as well as many very strong institutions in its overall network. It was essential for us to establish renewable energy. We made the whole thing a bit bigger at the Energy Park, where we developed the “satellite concept,” and say: We use everything from the tree. From the trunk to the chips. You get chips when you saw wood. We take these chips and dry them with the heat produced during electricity generation. This way, we ensure that the heat is used when it’s actually needed. Because an OAC turbine, and a power plant like that, has to work 8,760 hours a year. So we produce pellets for 8,760 hours. We store the heat in the pellets, which we use at the satellite cogeneration plant, where we start a combustion process with the pellets with reduction of oxygen, which then produces a combustible gas. This combustible gas then goes to cogeneration plants and generates heat and electricity there, at the point of sale, so to speak, i.e., at the citizen’s premises. 00:03:56,218 --&gt; 00:03:59,218 And so the cycle is again complete. And we have what I call integrated energy sector cycles, because the resource we have in our region is also fully used in the region. The first project done by the path of WUNsiedel now seems very, very small. It was the first citizens’ photovoltaic installation back in 2004. Back then, no bank would give us the money to finance such an installation. We mobilized local people and built no less than an 80 kW peak system – we laugh about it today – on the SWW building. Today, it still yields seven percent interest. Let’s take the subject of battery storage. We were firmly convinced that we needed flexibility. So we had a good think. What kind of market is out there? The primary regulation energy market. And with that in mind, we developed a business model for this lithium-ion storage system. In full knowledge of the fact that this system does something completely different when we need it: not frequency stabilization for the transmission network, but a starter battery for black start capability, then frequency stabilization in the local distribution network, but also peak shaving in the load changes, simply to balance the fluctuating photovoltaic and wind energy plants in the grid area of SWW Wunsiedel GmbH. And that’s what it’s doing now. Industry suddenly sits up and notices that the path of WUNsiedel, with this technology partnership, is actually a location advantage. As what happens when the lights go out in Europe? Wunsiedel’s still shining bright! And this partnership, this technology partnership, will make sure of it, with the expertise of Siemens AG and our dedication. We need to throw all of our inhibitions, burdens, and barriers to thinking overboard. And imagine: how would we design the energy supply system if we were the first to do so? We would make every building into a power plant. They’re all power plants, currently functioning as stand-alone units. But it’s really important to us that these units communicate with each other. Then along came Siemens AG and said, “Fine, we have it all as a cloud solution.” With AI, i.e., artificial intelligence, with fuzzy logic, processes that adjust themselves. We don’t have any server problems; we know that the data we use is protected. We know that everything is tracked, and we can write our own applications. Building on this, we will now jointly address the question of the distribution network 4.0. In other words, what does the distribution network of the future look like? How much intelligence does it need? Which stations actually need to be smart? And most importantly, how does all this communicate with the market? Wunsiedel doesn’t want to be self-sufficient; that’s not the goal. But we want to be able to be self-sufficient if we need to be. And that, in turn, gives our people peace of mind, no matter what happens. Energy, and with it water supplies and sewage disposal, is secured even in the event of a disaster. It’s our job, and we want to do it well. Our most recent project is much bigger, as well as much more important. It concerns the long-term storage system. How can we transport the surplus energy we have in summer over long periods? It’s only possible with the molecule. That’s why WUN H2 was established, and we hope to start building the Silyzer 300 this year. The first, and I hope also the biggest, hydrogen production facility using PEM electrolysis in Germany. And all this in little Wunsiedel. The path of WUNsiedel is never-ending. Even when you’ve done it all once, you go through it again and will always find something new.
