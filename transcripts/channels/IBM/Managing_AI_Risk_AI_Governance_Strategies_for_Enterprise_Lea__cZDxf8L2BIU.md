## 스크립트

There's no escaping that AI is a complete revolution and of course, that applies to security as well. I think the biggest blind spot in governance right now is the perception that having to comply with governance is going to slow down that AI adoption process, when in actuality it's an enabler. When we think about AI in cybersecurity, we think about it really in two ways. The first one is that how we use AI to really enhance how we can defend against bad actors. But equally, those bad actors also have access to AI, which is going to enhance their capability to launch destructive attacks against us, the defenders. We've been used to for many years seeing phishing emails that are often poorly written. And now, of course, a lot of what we see when it comes to phishing is really well-constructed grammatically correct emails that go a long way to help the threat actors. That's a direct result of AI. AI is being used by threat actors to rewrite phishing emails to make them look a lot more credible. One of the other areas that we see AI being used a lot by malicious actors is in deepfakes. You know, there's one well-known attack where you actually see a virtual meeting where an individual has been injected into that meeting using a deepfake, to pose as a CFO. It's important that organizations continue to invest in guardrails and keep ahead of the new threats. Things like detecting for jailbreaking, prompt poisoning, hallucinations, drift and bias. When an organization has detailed policies and procedures, those can be operationalized using technology. This helps to ensure that as AI is adopted anywhere within the organization, be it a business unit or a different geography, they're working through automated workflows that enforce the best practice as it was defined in their policies. Organizations really do need to think through about who is responsible for AI security. It's not just a security job. It's about how you bring your governance, your security, and the runtime, IT organizations and the business units together to understand how AI is going to be applied and used, not just in the enterprise itself, but through the supply chain as well. And it's only by coordinating all those elements that you can really have an effective security response. One of the most important pieces of an AI governance program is employee training and education. Similar to data privacy, AI governance needs to be something that is part of every employee's job so that they understand the responsibilities around using AI effectively, in addition to pursuing that innovation opportunity. Shadow AI is similar to shadow IT. It's technology working within the organization that hasn't gone through the approval process prior to being used and deployed to production. That's where technology can help to automatically identify AI that's not in inventory. Then apply risk assessments, understand what's needed to keep the AI in compliance, and make sure that effective controls are applied. In our study, The Cost of a Data Breach Report, we found that the actual cost of data breaches have gone down. And there are a number of reasons for that. One is firstly that we are getting better, and we're now beginning to deploy things like AI tools into the defense landscape. That means that we sped up and that has resulted in us being able to get ahead of the attackers and stop these things turning into really bad outcomes. The opportunity is to maintain focus not on innovation, but on optimizing the controls that helped protect the technology that organizations are investing in. This includes good risk management controls, proactive audits that aren't focused on just a few times a year, and getting a view to risks real time through security tools that can provide that awareness and really mitigate the impact of a data breach that could result in loss events, reputational risk, or terrible customer experiences for an organization. But the one thing that is really important to bear in mind is that the threat actors are getting better all the time as well. So we have to improve. In security, we're about continuous improvement. Adopt AI aggressively. Look at how the whole enterprise works, not just the security piece of it. Think about those things and that will mean that hopefully the organization that you might be in doesn't become another statistic in that report.
