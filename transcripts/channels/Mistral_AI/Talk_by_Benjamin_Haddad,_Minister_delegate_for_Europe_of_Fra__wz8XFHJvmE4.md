And um you know, as I was preparing here when we think about AI, we know the the incredible opportunities in terms of industrial acceleration, in terms of innovation, in terms of transformation of our lifestyle, of our labor market, of our transportation, of our scientific progress. The question is whether we want Chinese and American AI or whether we want Europeans to have their own actors. And for a long time, if I want to speak a bit frankly about this issue, the EU saw itself as what they called a regulatory power or normative power. The idea was that others would create the innovation, would create the startups, and we would write the rules. The toolkit for how to use this innovation, how to, you know, fight against disinformation, protect the information space, fight fight against the manipulation of algorithm, etc., etc. But the truth is the one that creates and shapes the norm, the one that fabricates how we use this technology and how we think about it is the innovator. And if Europe wants to be a technological power, wants to set its own rules for the use of technology, for its information space, for how our democracies work, it needs innovation. It needs to invest massively in cutting-edge innovation. And this is why I'm here and I've seen all the proposals that Mistral has put forth, the 22 proposals for Europe to be a technological superpower, for Europe to be sovereign, to reduce its dependencies on external actors when it comes intelligence. And I want to tell you how much we support this agenda and how much concretely we're pushing it to unfold today at the European level. First and foremost, you need massive investment, public and private investment. When you compare Europe to the United States, we're under-investing, we're under-leveraged. And so, what we need to do and we'll have big debates in the next few months, for example, on the next European budget, what we call the multiannual framework. You know, very often when I talk to entrepreneurs, when I go to Station F and elsewhere, I ask them a very basic question. What does Europe represent for you? And when you think about it, you should have the most pro-European crowd. You have people who are entrepreneurs, who took risks, who innovate, who are interested in the world. But very often they tell me bureaucratic complexities, too much administrative paperwork and red tape, and an internal market that is still too fragmented. You have companies that have been successful in France. They already have customers. They already have investors. But they tell me if I want to go tomorrow to Germany, to Poland, to Spain, or to Sweden, I feel I have to restart everything from scratch. Different regulatory environment, different banking environment, not the same relationships, not the same insurance, etc., etc. And this is why we've launched a big effort that needs to accelerate to streamline our regulation, to simplify our rule. There's been a few omnibuses, CSRD, CSDDD, a digital omnibus to really simplify a lot of the text that we put forth in the last few years in Europe. And this is why we're working also on creating the same rules for everyone. For example, what we call the 20th regime EU Inc that I'm sure some of you have heard about. It's very simply put, the idea that you could create your own company in 48 hours, completely dematerialized, the same way and recognized everywhere in Europe. It's concretely the saving and investment union to make sure that we have the same supervisory authorities across the European continent, the same rules, so we have the depth of market to invest in in Europe. This is the agenda that we're pushing at the European level with the European Commission, precisely to make sure that we can leverage the incredible opportunity that our internal market, 450 million people. And that's absolutely critical in the area of artificial intelligence.
