## 스크립트

Your body has many systems that keep you active and healthy. One of these systems helps balance sugar. And if you have type 2 diabetes, it means that you have higher than the target levels of sugar. You might be thinking, why is balancing sugar important? Sugar is your body's main source of energy and comes from the food you eat. When you eat a meal, sugar levels are usually at their highest. Sugar in your food powers your brain, muscles, and other body parts. But when you eat more sugar than your body needs, it stores the leftovers in your liver and fat tissue, saving it for energy at a later time. If you hadn't eaten enough and your body needs more fuel, it can use some that you have stored. This balancing act keeps sugar levels within target ranges all day long. For someone with type 2 diabetes, a target range is between 80 and 130 before meals or less than 180 1 to 2 hours after meals. However, having type 2 diabetes means that it's hard for your body to keep these sugar levels balanced. As the disease progresses, it becomes even harder for your body to use and store sugar from the food you eat. This puts sugar levels outside of the target range and knocks your body out of balance. Over time, this imbalance can impact your weight and the health of your eyes, kidneys, feet, and heart. Diet and exercise are important and help your body keep sugar levels balanced. This is why improving them is a major part of treating type 2 diabetes. However, when diet and exercise aren't enough, your doctor will talk to you about adding medicine to your treatment plan.
