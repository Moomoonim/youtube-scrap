## 스크립트

There was this instance where my dad bought a brand new TV, and the first thing he said when the TV got delivered into the house was, “Yinka, do not open this television.” I'm Yinka. I'm a software development manager here at IBM Infrastructure, based out of Poughkeepsie, New York. I work with IBM Z and Z is the platform that runs over 95% of the world’s credit card transactions and also over 87% of the ATM transactions. Growing up, I was very curious about electronics and I'm hearing audio coming out of a device, I'm like, “How is that happening?” And so, I’ll always open up electronic gadgets around the house. Based out of my curiosity, I always walk around the aisles. And then, I look into the bin where we recycle old computers or old electronics. And over time, I find amazing technology in those bins to get recycled. So I go around, I see cool stuff here and there. I take it back to my office, put some parts to make it functional, and then I donate it to different causes like people that want to functionally use it or people that want to keep it for the historical reasons. Being at IBM for over ten years, I’ve switched roles for about four times. And so I was in Power Systems. I was doing software architecture, moved into Z, software development, moved into performance, and then moved into management. So I've been able to explore a huge part of IBM within ten years, and there's still so much to learn, right? So now we are breaking into AI. IBM is the market leader in AI, and also we're a leader in quantum. So, there's a lot to learn at IBM. We have a geographically distributed team. And we've been building more skills here based out of Poughkeepsie. Traditionally, the product is over 50 years old. So we have folks that have over 40 years of experience in IBM. And then bringing those new hires and integrating them into the product and also bringing them up to speed, training them. We’re building the next generation that is going to continue to propel the product forward. We're doing this in a very collaborative manner and ensuring that they're all happy to be part of this journey.
