## 스크립트

You know, our approach to visual effects is lean and mean. We're a small company. That said, we still specialize in really complex, high end work. We just don't do it at the scale that a larger company does. We're able to compete and show work that is world renowned, Oscar winning work. The Studio did present quite a few challenges for us. They tried to complete the entire episodes as sort of one shots or “oners”. We were the lead visual effects vendor for that show. We had one sequence in particular. Madness unfolded when a person threw a burrito at the other, and it culminated in the entire, like, collapse of a set. I did all the simulations there, the structure and how it was going to collapse. That was a big shot for the series. I think the shot involved, like, 20 different CG digi-doubles. All of that has to be believable. And then it has to collapse and completely fall apart in a way that you, you also believe. It was really exciting and really fun. And I think it came off really, really well. Because we're small, it's really important to utilize all the resources we have. We don't have a huge budget for unlimited processors or unlimited render nodes. The only way we can pull that stuff off is if we have stuff that is just made to crush 3D renders. Solutions that we've found that work really well for us are the Threadripper and Lenovo workstations. One of the great things about Threadripper is we amplify our render pool by two, just because the hardware so powerful and it can accommodate it. So we’d be running GPU renders and CPU renders at the same time. Everybody talks about efficiency. But efficiency for us means that you save time to put that last bit into the finishing of something. And it makes work that lasts, you can look at again and again and again and just say like, “Wow. It's...it's great.”
