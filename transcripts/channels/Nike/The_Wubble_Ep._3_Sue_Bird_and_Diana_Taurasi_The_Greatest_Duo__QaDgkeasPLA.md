## 스크립트

2020 started off great. For myself personally in like a perfect dream world. I would've thought you play the 2020 WNBA season, you play the Olympics, you hopefully win a gold, you hopefully win a championship, see you later. What a great way to go out that clearly wasn't an option. 2020 was a year of a lot of change and for all of us to look at things differently, look in the mirror as a woman's basketball player it changed the course of our league. USA Basketball put together this huge tour all in the name of generating hype around women's basketball. And what was also happening was our CBA was getting signed. So we've now got this like epic CBA, we killed it, we got all this money, all arrows are like pointing in the right direction for women's basketball. Nike's stepping up in this huge way where they're really gonna promote our team. Like we're gonna be the focal point of all these things, and the pandemic took it all away. I think all of us collectively we're like, are you kidding me? Now, we don't know what the hell is gonna happen. If we don't play, if we don't have anything, what happens for women's basketball? And then you have the social reckoning that happens. We all see George Floyd get murdered. We all hear about Breonna Taylor getting murdered. Every time you turn on the news, it's in your face. And simultaneously we're trying to figure out if we can have a season amongst all of this. From there, it then became all right, well, if we're gonna have a season it needs to stand for social justice. We're not playing just to play basketball. That was like the one non-negotiable in every conversation we had. The season was gonna be in honor of Breonna Taylor, in honor of Say Her Name. Leading up into what ended up being the Wubble season. There was all the negotiations that went in to putting on that season. Those negotiations were wild, a lot of raw emotion and this is really where I like fully and truly learned there was gonna be moments where I was gonna need to be quiet. There was certain conversations that I just had no place in putting my voice. So it was just about trying to figure out how to show up and when the right time was to do it. And the one thing I always say is like, it's never perfect, you make mistakes, you know? But it doesn't mean you you don't keep showing up. I mean, this goes back to transferring to Christ the King. Sports I think just naturally gives you so much exposure to so many different people. Whether it's race, religion, you name it, sexual orientation, right? It's like a melting pot in those locker rooms. I also understood I could help my friends or my family or whatever conversation I was in even if it's just you and one other person you can always create change. It's not just because we're WNBA players on ESPN with the media platform we have, no, that's not the only way to create change. And then at the same time, having my own realizations of the privilege that I have as a white person, how I can now use that. Once we had established what our non-negotiable was, the league had to figure out how it worked for them financially, logistically, and so once all of that got finished, the best part from a player's standpoint was we got the season to stand for social justice, we got 100% of our money and we got through a bubble season in a pandemic. If I had to say one statement about it, I would say, I feel as if this shift in 2020 now just allows really all of women's sports to get the shot they've always deserved. The reality of the WNBA is that we're majority Black, we're women, right? We have trans players. So it's this melting pot of all the marginalized groups we see in our world. And for a very long time, I personally feel those were the reasons that we never had a fair chance at the corporate sponsorship money, at the media coverage. It was just, we were always able to be held at an arms length maybe every now and then some scraps were thrown our way so people could check a box, but it was never like a real investment. And like I said, I think the world changed. And with that, with that change came more visibility for our league. And the best part about it is the proofs in the pudding, like the reality is people like us. And I always say the story of the WNBA is it's that we've never been truly invested in and we're still f*ckin here.
