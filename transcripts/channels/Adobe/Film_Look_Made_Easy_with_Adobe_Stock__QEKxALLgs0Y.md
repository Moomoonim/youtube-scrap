Film looks are back. Remaking the Film Look is one of the most trending styles on social media. For a pretty long time now. It's often pretty difficult to find the right assets to create the perfect film look that we want to go for. Well, fortunately, Adobe Stock makes this process significantly easier. So in this video, let's turn this travel film into this nostalgic aesthetic masterpiece using assets from Adobe Stock. First things first. Let's find film letterbox. Now, letterbox is a blank space that you will have within the frame. And especially for the film look, we needed a round corner with certain esthetic onto it. Technically, you can make this letterbox, but first it's very time consuming. And second, there's certain effects within different overlays in Adobe Stock that makes it just a bit more special. I found this gritty film letterbox overlay in the website. Once you licensed the effect and download it from the website, all you need to do is to open project file within Premiere Pro, import that overlay into your project. You don't really have to change the blend mode or anything I want you to do is to adjust the size of the overlay for the resolution of the project file. I love this letterbox overlay because it has a certain amount of grain that is around the frame, which makes it just a bit more, you know, a little bit more special. Since this video is going to be 16x9 horizontal film. This overlay works perfectly. Second asset will be film grain. Now, film grain is a type of effect that naturally occurs when you're shooting film. But since we're filming in digital, we have to add an overlay on top of our footage. I found this super realistic grain in 4K, which works perfectly for this video since this is a MOV file. You applied this film grain footage on top of your cinematic footage, and change the blend mode to soft light. Not only you will get the grain that you need it, but it will bring the exposure of the footage just a bit. Making the looks of your footage just a bit more soft. Sometimes I like to add a different type of grain overlays to have just a bit more character. I found this damage to film overlay from the website, so once again I got the license I downloaded. Open the project file and import type of the grain that I like to implement in the footage, and I change the blend mode to lighten. And there you go. Now we have a just a bit amount of grain to have, just a bit of touch, and there are a ton of different types of film grain and Adobe Stock, so you shouldn't have any problem. Third, let's add some light leaks. I personally love to use light leaks as a transition, because I think it's a cool way to have transition, and with the sound effects, it will look pretty dynamic too. I got this light leak transition within Premiere Pro's Adobe Stock Library, and all I needed to do is license the graphic within Premiere Pro and drag the effect onto the timeline, change the background of the timeline, change the blend mode to lighten and boom. I have this beautiful light, the transition that I can just use it right in Premiere Pro, and that's pretty much it for the basic film look. However, if you want to go above and beyond, there are a couple of film look that I could use for After Effects to have this more aggressive film look that you can try.
