- The use of AI by consumers in health care is the biggest, fastest change we've seen in hundreds of years in how people access health care. So what I'm really interested in is not just what it means in the short term for consumers today, but what it's going to mean for our entire health system. - Ben, we know that people are using these tools to check symptoms, but I suppose, what comes next? So we're going to go from a world where, you know, consumers are just, you know, corresponding with large language models, checking their symptoms, understanding their diagnoses, et cetera, to a role where they have a health agent, an always-on health agent that not only can they, you know, ask it questions, but they can connect it to their data. Whether it's from their wearables or whether they, you know, grant it permission to have access to their results and their health records, et cetera. And it can create a really sort of integrated, always on health advisor that gives you advice about diet, about exercise, about sleep, about all those things that are actually, you know, genuinely related to your underlying health conditions and circumstances. And then, you know, it potentially can move from there to make appointments for you, to, you know, arrange health care checkups, to prompt you to get your immunizations, prompt you and make appointments for you to do things that are important for your health. I think that sort of always on health advisor function is a really interesting one and will, you know, there's some really interesting questions about whether that's something which, you know, in nationalized health systems, is provided by that national health system. I think there's some challenging questions about how those agents will access private and confidential data and what sort of security and confidentiality will be provided alongside that.
