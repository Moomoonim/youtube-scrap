## 스크립트

Hello, my name is Sydnee Thompson. I am from Dallas, Texas, and the concept of my sneaker was “Threads of Survival.” I'm Bryce Wong, .SWOOSH Virtual Footwear and Collections Designer. Threads of Survival is essentially an expression of my story. I am a cancer survivor. I've been through a lot of different things– ups and downs, and I think we all have in some way. So this design was literally an expression of the joys of survival, but also recognizing that everything is not always, you know, just so smooth as we get through this thing that we call life and through those rough parts that we find those small silver linings or gold linings. Yeah, I think your shoe is one of the ones that has that personal feel that like really draws you in it’s the details that makes you ask, “Hey, what is this all about?” “And what does this mean?” We can start with your favorite detail on the shoe. I think if I had to pick is some of those that just tie back to what I call my survival tribe. So growing up in a family of five. Mom, dad, sister, brother, I'm the middle middle child. The five of us are represented and within the middle of the five perfs are two that are highlighted in gold, and those gold recognize my new survival tribe, which is my husband and my baby. Bringing the whole squad along. Yeah, yeah. No, I love that. I'll tell you my favorite detail. It's the cherry candy dubrae. That was something that was super fun. Just because, when are you going to put that on a real shoe. Mmhmm. That's the beauty. That was the beauty of, like, working in the virtual space where you can just do anything. That particular piece, I think, is so interesting because, like, it came out of this story of us talking through things in the session. So it wasn't a picture of it, it wasn't written in my caption, but it was a very distinct part of my story as a cancer kid. When I was 11 or 12 years old, I was diagnosed with Stage 4B Hodgkin Lymphoma. When I was 12. I had my first bald birthday, because like chemo wipe my hair out like within the first three weeks. And one of the medicines that I would take would change the taste in your mouth. And so my grandmother would always come to my hospital visits and she would bring me a bag of hard candies. And at the time I did not like the cherry flavor. And so I would always like have every other flavor would eat them throughout that medicine and it would help. And so after going through that experience, I didn't like them anymore because it reminded me of the medicine. Now the candies tasted like the medicine was like, how does this even happen? Now I only like the cherry version of that Candy. March 19, 2004, I was declared cancer free, so I had done my nine months of chemotherapy. 319 is like a second birthday and seeing that remission date next to a Nike check is like, what is happening?
