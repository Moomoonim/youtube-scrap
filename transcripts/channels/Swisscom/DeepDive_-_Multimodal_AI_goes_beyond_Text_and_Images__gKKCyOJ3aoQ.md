## 스크립트

Once again, the new GPT-4o model is turning everything upside down. At the moment, two AI trends are exploding across social media. The so‑called starter packs, where personalities and their accessories are shown as action figures, and pictures in the style of the Japanese Studio Ghibli. Behind these trends lies GPT‑4o and a new technology that enables text creation and image editing with a simple prompt: multimodality. In this video, we’ll take a look what multimodal models can do, how you use them, and where their limits lie. Imagine a top chef who has vegetables, meat, and dairy stored in the fridge. For a multi‑course meal, she takes a bit of everything and cooks up a superb menu. Multimodal AI works similarly. Instead of ingredients, it keeps text and images in the same compartment. The word “cat” and a photo of a cat sit there side by side. Later, if you ask for “a cat playing the violin,” the model immediately combines the shape of the cat and a violin and creates a fitting result. Simply put: multimodal AI understands the relationship between image and text far better than pure text or image AIs. Under the hood, it runs on what’s called a transformer model. It converts text and images into numbers and brings them into one shared semantic space. Once an image needs to be generated, the autoregressive decoder steps in. It constructs the picture step by step, and each new part follows the previous one. So in the end, everything fits perfectly, exactly the way the prompt demanded. Right now, three multimodal AI tools are in the spotlight. GPT‑4o by OpenAI processes pictures and text almost in real time. Its focus is on visual understanding, it can talk to you through a voice mode if you want, and it powers the current AI image trends. Gemini 2.0 Flash by Google is all about speed. It’s already built into Android, Google Search, and YouTube tools, and reacts quickly when you ask further questions or want something else. Claude 3 from Anthropic can analyze whole PDFs, tables, and slide decks at once, and promises that your chat data isn’t used for training. Claude is also very popular for coding. All three handle several media types, but each puts a different emphasis in the implementation. Let’s test that in practice, using GPT‑4o as an example. By the way: GPT‑4o is also available for free, with some restrictions, on chatgpt.com. So you can follow along right now. Hmm, let's have ChatGPT propose me a good story. Give me an example of a comic story I can tell in three panels. Great, let’s take that! To make it a bit more personal, I’ll upload a photo of myself. Little disclaimer: remember, only upload pictures, whether of yourself or others, that you have permission for and would otherwise share on social media. Make the story in a comic‑style with me as the character. Haha, nice! Apart from two or three small details it already looks really great. Now I can refine it with more prompts until my comic looks the way I want. The conversational aspect really is a amazing. After every prompt I can ask follow‑up questions or correct something, and the AI still knows exactly what we discussed before. Still, it's helpful to keep the basic structure of image‑AI prompting guides. Want to learn more about prompting? You’ll find a link to that video in the description. The biggest advantage is, how precisely GPT‑4o responds to prompts. It often applies font, layout, and color schemes exactly as you want. And you can make changes with a single sentence. Pictures and characters also stay consistent across multiple scenes. That's something classic diffusion models achieve only with great effort. Still, there are weaknesses too. For photorealistic images without text, traditional image models, like Midjourney for example, are sometimes better. OpenAI themselves say, that GPT‑4o sometimes cuts off image edges or makes very small lettering slightly blurry. Plus, this kind of image generation needs more computing power and much more energy. Hence it takes longer until your picture is ready. When it comes to data, ChatGPT is like a social network. Everything you type — whether you use the free or paid version — can by default be used for training purposes. In the menu under “Settings” → “Data Controls”, you’ll find the switch labeled “Model improvements.” Only after you turn it off, will your new chats no longer be used for training. We still recommend: only upload pictures and information that you would share on a social network. Multimodal AIs are the next generation of generative AI.
