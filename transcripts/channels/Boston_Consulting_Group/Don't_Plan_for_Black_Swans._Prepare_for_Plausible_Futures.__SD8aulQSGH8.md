## 스크립트

When I speak with leaders around the world about the future, they ask me about dramatic events. How do I prepare for a breakthrough in nuclear fusion or war in low orbit? But these aren't the scenarios that leaders should be focusing on. Black Swan events are, by their nature, very low probability and should not anchor your organization's strategy. Instead, leaders should be preparing for futures that are genuinely on the table, backed by data with strategic choices to make today. For our latest report, we developed four scenarios for the year 2050 that are contrasting but plausible. Imagine a world where global trade falls back to Cold War levels, where defense spending nearly triples from today, and where the World Trade Organization no longer exists. That's our Battling Blocs scenario, and it's built from trends already in motion today. Contrast this scenario with AI Abundance, where multilateral cooperation on technology regulation drives deep global integration. Both are potential futures. Both require very different strategies. The decisions leaders make in the next five years must be informed by rigorous foresight, as they will shape the next 25.
