## 스크립트

At Pixar, we have an expression: the art challenges the technology, and the technology inspires the art. And faster hardware with more memory allows us to do things we couldn't do before. I always describe RenderMan as being the thing that does all the math, and the physics that actually gives you each color of each pixel across your entire film or short. At Pixar films are really complex, and the scenes are enormous, and they require a lot of memory. RenderMan’s been rendering on CPUs for a long time. We're entering a new phase with RenderMan XPU, where we're using GPU acceleration to deliver new kinds of images. If you look at the recent cards that have been coming out, the Adas, and now the Blackwells, the memory limitations aren't there anymore. 99% of Pixar shots will now fit on a Blackwell. One of the most exciting things that's happening is we’re going to be rendering a portion of Toy Story 5 with XPU. This is going to give the artists greater flexibility, more efficiency, and it's going to make our render farm more efficient at the same time. One of the areas where we'll see the biggest impact of this technology of XPU and GPU acceleration is in lighting. Lighting’s always been a bottleneck because it takes so long to adjust the lights to get them to look good. The ability to work faster allows the lighters to do more shots more efficiently, and actually apply the art of cinematography, instead of worrying about the technical tidbits. To me, what's really exciting about our partnership with NVIDIA is I think it goes to the root of what Pixar is about. We are always trying to push the boundaries of technology in art. That helps us tell better stories. It allows us to take people to worlds they haven't seen before, and to introduce them to characters that may not have been possible just a few years prior.
