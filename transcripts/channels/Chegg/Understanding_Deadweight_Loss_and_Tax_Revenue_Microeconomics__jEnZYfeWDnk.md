(music) Remember, deadweight loss occurs when total surplus drops as a result of market distortion, such as a tax. Deadweight loss as a result of taxes will lead to loss of economic efficiency, since the equilibrium outcome is not achieved. (music) As tax increases, the amount of deadweight loss increases more rapidly than the size of the tax. The effect of tax increase on tax revenue is not direct. Initially, rising tax will lead to higher revenue. At some point, however, higher taxes will decrease tax revenue. In this graph, the initial tax is T dollar per unit. At this tax rate, the equilibrium quantity is Q1. The DWL is the area of the small triangle. If the tax doubles to 2T dollar per unit, the equilibrium quantity lowers to Q2, and the DWL now is more than double the initial deadweight loss. In fact, the new DWL, which is shown by the larger triangle, pink and orange shaded area, is now greater than the initial DWL by a factor of four. Think about it. If DWL is shown as a triangle the area depends on the square of its size. Doubling a tax doubles both its base and height, increasing the size of the deadweight loss by a factor of four. If the tax triples to 3T dollar per unit, the equilibrium quantity falls to Q3 and the DWL now becomes greater than the initial DWL by a factor of nine. Since the deadweight loss at 3T dollar is the area of the larger triangle, increasing tax will cost the DWL to increase by the square of the new tax size. At a tax of 3T dollar, the base and the height triples, so the deadweight loss rises by a factor of nine. This implies that raising tax rates when they are low causes minimal harm, while lowering them produces little benefit. With higher tax rates increases are harmful and reductions are extra helpful. The relationship between the tax size and the tax revenue, where higher tax size initially results in higher revenue, while increasingly higher tax size will start to lower. The tax revenue is summarized by Laffer Curve. The Laffer Curve is a line that shows the relationship between two variables, the size of the tax and the size of the tax revenue.
