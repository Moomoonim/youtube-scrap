Hello! Welcome to a new episode where we talk about Schneider Electric solutions. In previous episodes, we talked about smart homes, comfort, and energy efficiency. But how smart is a home if it is not also safe? I have Remus here with me. Remus, welcome! Thank you! Everyone is talking about modernizing a home's electrical installations, but the truth is that many homes still use protection devices that are 20 to 30 years old. Why is this the right time to talk about electrical safety? Because our homes are no longer the same as they were 20 or 30 years ago. The equipment in our homes is completely different. We now have induction hobs, refrigerators, washing machines with inverter technology, and inverter air conditioners. So these are pieces of equipment with specific protection requirements. The Easy9 Pro range is a natural evolution of the Easy9 range, and it is an evolution that addresses these new requirements. And why do you believe this evolution of protection devices is necessary? You said it is an evolution of the Easy9 range. Indeed, an evolution is necessary because electrical installations have evolved, because today's installations are much more complex, in line with the far more advanced equipment we now have in our homes, equipment that has specific protection requirements. At the same time, Romanian legislation has also evolved, so that regulations now impose different requirements that were not previously in place. I understand. Does that mean the old breakers in homes are no longer sufficient? Exactly. The new Easy9 Pro range brings new features, aligned with the new requirements of modern equipment. What is new about Easy9 Pro in terms of home safety? First of all, Easy9 Pro comes with a higher breaking capacity. It offers a breaking capacity of 6,000 amps, compared to 4,500 amps for the current Easy9 range. Then, in the new range, we will have both B curves and C curves, and this will apply to both MCBs and RCBs. Could you explain a little what that means so everyone can understand C and B tripping curves? The tripping curve is the threshold at which the breaker trips instantaneously. Normally, a breaker, let's say, has a rated current of 10 amps. If we exceed those 10 amps and that circuit carries 12 to 15 amps, then the breaker will trip, but it will not trip instantaneously. It will trip as the breaker heats up — a thermal trip. If we exceed it by a lot, if we have a short circuit and instead of 10 amps, the current reaches 100 amps, then the trip is instantaneous and it is an electromagnetic trip, instantaneous. This threshold, when we talk about a C curve, this instantaneous threshold is somewhere between 5 and 8 times the rated current. So for a 10-amp breaker, it is somewhere between 50 and 80 amps. A B-curve breaker has this instantaneous trip threshold much lower. It is between 3 and 5 times the rated current, meaning between 30 and 50 amps. So it trips at a slightly lower current. It does not trip at 100 amps, it will trip at 30 to 40 amps. It will trip instantaneously. That is the difference between B curve and C curve. So the simple way to put it is that B-curve devices trip faster. Yes, that is a very important aspect, especially given the pace of life today and how overloaded we are — and not just us, but also the home's electrical installation. So thank you for explaining it in a way the public can understand. Where do you think Easy9 Pro is best suited, and what recommendations would you give in this regard? So that people can better understand this range and make the right choices. Of course, the Easy9 Pro range remains suitable for absolutely all residential installations, but the new features, the higher breaking capacity, and the new types of devices mean that this equipment, this Easy9 Pro range, can also be installed in commercial buildings, office spaces, stores, supermarkets, workshops — wherever protection is needed, Easy9 Pro can be there to provide it. Remus, thank you for all these details, and thank you for explaining everything in such an easy-to-understand way.
