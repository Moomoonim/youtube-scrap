## 스크립트

Your child has an Android smartphone. And you would like to actively influence screen time and the content they consume. Google offers its own solution for this, called Family Link. To do this, you have to do two things. First, you need to create a Google account for your child. And also own one yourself. Secondly, you can set screen time in the app, limit content, purchases and apps. Now I'll show you step by step how to do it. First, download the FamilyLink app from the Play or App Store. When you open the app, you'll need to log in with a Google account. For the FamilyLink to work, your child also needs a Google account. Click Yes if your child already has an account, or No if your child doesn't already have one. Click Continue to create a child account. First, enter your child's first and last name, then the date of birth and gender. Now you can create a new Gmail address at the bottom. Since many Gmail addresses are already taken, you may need to try a few options until you find one that is still available. Did you make it? You can create a strong password for your child. Make a strong password that you can both remember. It's best to store it in a password manager so that it doesn't get lost. You can find out more about secure passwords under the link in the description. Now the parent reads the information from Google carefully and confirms it if you agree. Click on "Continue" to continue with the account opening. If you have not yet deposited a means of payment, you must do so to prove that you are the person entitled to the relationship. The first step has been taken. Your child's account is ready. Now pick up your child's device to connect it to Family Link. In the settings, click on "Digital wellbeing" and "Parental controls". Now you have to log in to your parent account. In the last step, you can now set the first settings for your child's device. In list form, you can now see all the apps installed on your child's smartphone. Deselect all the ones you want to delete and click "Next" to complete the process. Now you can set general daily limits and rest periods. If the daily limit is reached, you would have the option to set bonus times for your child. Under Content restrictions, you can control what Google Apps can see. YouTube and Chrome are especially important, where you can choose to only allow your child to see age-appropriate videos and search results. Once you've made the arrangements, you can click on "Location settings" under "Account settings" to share your child's device's location with you. To get an overview of your child's device usage, you can click on "Top Activities" in the app. So, now your child's Android device is ready. I recommend that you discuss the settings with your child so that they know exactly why what is being restricted and how they can handle the device safely. But remember, it is important that you accompany your child closely with their first mobile phone, especially at the beginning. You can find tips on how to do this in our digital guide to the first mobile phone for my child on Swisscom Campus. I'm Marcel and I wish you and your child a lot of fun with the device.
