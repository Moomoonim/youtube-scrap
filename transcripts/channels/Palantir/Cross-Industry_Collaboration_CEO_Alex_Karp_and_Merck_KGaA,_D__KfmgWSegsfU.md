I'm happy to be here with you. We’re here in our Munich office, where we work together in one of the most important areas of the world. I wanted to ask: what is it that are we doing together? Why is it interesting and why is it so complicated? Alex, I am really happy to be here today, and that we are talking together about a very important topic that is on everybody's mind. Semiconductors are a very important topic at the moment. I'm Kai Beckmann from Merck KGaA in Darmstadt, Germany. And we have been working together for a very long time, many, many years - and recently on the topic of semiconductors. Yes, and we came together in another area. So, semiconductors, most people think there's a semiconductor produced somehow, much like there's milk in cows. But, making semiconductors is one of the most complicated things in the world. How are they made and why is it complicated? And why is it helpful to work together? It's pleasant to see you. We like to work with you. But why do we need to work together? What are the complexities and why does the whole world depend on semiconductors? I think this year everybody realized- well, it started last year- that suddenly semiconductors became scarce and suddenly semiconductors became important for everybody, for all people. And semiconductors are not an interchangeable object, there are only a few. There’s a conception that I can take this one or that one and then my car still works, but, rather, they are highly specialized components of which there are thousands of different kinds and are manufactured using very different technologies by different companies and then fulfill a very special purpose. In a car, they ensure the navigation works, that you can drive automatically, that the airbag opens at the right moment, that the window regulators work. In a mobile phone, they take over all the functions; each of these semiconductors is highly specialized and consists of billions of transistors in the processor. A processor in a mobile phone today has over ten billion transistors, which all have to work together to function and are all very, very small. We are talking about nanometers. They are very, very small structures and it takes three months to make a chip like that. It's not like you put one in the machine in the morning and it's ready in the evening; it takes three months from the beginning of the process to the end of the process with thousands of steps in between. And what do we do together and how is Foundry used? Why would you need software to make semiconductors? What is the problem? We now know, thanks to your explanation, that we basically can't have life as we know it without semiconductors. But why software? Why your expertise? What is the added value? Or why is it important that we come together? And what will we as a society get out of it? The production of semiconductors is done with very complex equipment, and requires a lot of highly specialized materials and chemicals -- several thousand different ones are used in semiconductor technology today. Perhaps you can imagine it vividly. We all liked chemistry at school or didn't like it so much. If you take the periodic table of the elements, half of it is used for semiconductor production. Half of this periodic table is used, and these materials are highly specialized, highly pure. They have to be brought together to produce the semiconductors across the various process steps. And why do we use Foundry together? Because, out of these individual process steps, we get billions of datasets about purity, the quality of the materials, temperature, pressure and various things. By optimizing these parameters, we can improve the semiconductor manufacturing process, improving our yield. In other words, more chips work in the end, and every decimal place in the yield is a gain for the manufacturer, of course. Making things cheaper and better for the environment -- because the process produces less waste. With Foundry, I create a better impact for the environment. Sustainability is an important point. And I can test new technologies faster because I don't need as many cycles to test new technologies. These are all effects that are very important for our customers – for the semiconductor manufacturers. … and the world. When supply chains have to be rebuilt in complicated times, a process that is so complicated that you can hardly optimize it in normal times, then we have a world where the cars don't work, the heating doesn't work, the computers that power everything, don't work And that's bad. It's not just an academic thing; it's a thing that we all basically depend on. And the primary strength is that it's not just two partners collaborating – one manufacturer and one material supplier - there are different equipment manufacturers and different material manufacturers. It's “many to many”. Many people work together and a common data platform is, of course, incredibly helpful, because the exchange is standardized and does not always have to be negotiated individually between two partners. Yes, that is also something that I think most people don't understand. The cooperation of many businesses is a prerequisite for building a semiconductor, and that also requires that the data is all in a form that can be exchanged, that can be handled operationally. And then you need know-how, which is also very rare, and software that can back it up. An important point on the data is that these connections are not obvious. The specialists – and there are really highly trained and highly focused specialists working – they naturally can't detect all these connections that we can make using Foundry. That was a surprise in the end. We were surprised which parameters of the materials suddenly influenced the effectiveness. That's a whole new way of looking at things that we wouldn't otherwise have been able to do. Yes, it's interesting that in a lot of things we're active, the lay perspective is that it's all easily producible – one hundred percent of the time. Science has progressed so far, has come so far, that one can just determine the parameters beforehand. But we're not there yet. With these complex processes, there's a lot of room for improvement and we simply need these advances now, because the network is suddenly more complicated for political reasons. Exactly. There are more players, more ingredients that then play a role. And, of course, the danger of having, I think the Americans call it confirmation bias –…. So you have an assumption and you try to confirm it and when you look at the data, suddenly a completely different view comes along. And that's what our researchers have seen, that you see things very, very differently when you look at the data. But thank you very much. This has meant a lot to us, to work with you for years, to deliver our platform to you . This collaboration with you and the fact that this plays such a big role and also that a lot of businesses will participate in it, motivates us at Palantir and is a big motivation for me personally. It’s a great thing. Thank you very much. Very briefly – the fact that we worked together during the pandemic without people meeting in person, I found particularly inspiring that that worked. We worked across how many countries… across three continents Asia, Europe and North America during the pandemic without people getting on a plane once. This also shows how well the teams worked. Many thanks for that. It was a very inspiring collaboration and still is, because we are always working to improve it further. Thank you, Alex. Thank you.
