## 스크립트

- You've got to take some big, bold steps. Understand that it's not going to be perfect along the way. - Jonathan, when you look at your marketing and comms function, my understanding is that you've viewed GenAI as the center and that the changes that you would make would be around GenAI, not something that you would just layer on top of it. - Correct. - And from the research I've done, 88% of people who sit in the comms leadership seat feel like they can't transform in that way. So tell us a little bit about what does closing that gap look like to you? - You know, I think part of why we're taking this approach is because if you really started from scratch, what would you want to do today? So what we've done internally is we've looked at all the processes across the organization, and we have spent time understanding how those processes work, who owns a process, and then which of those processes can you either automate, have AI touch it or impact it, change, get rid of, or certain instances you have to, you'll leave them the same. But, Russell, I think it's really important that if you don't lay out a goal that's substantial, even if you're not going to get, even if you don't have full confidence you can get there, if you just say, "Hey, we're going to go do 10% of AI in the organization," you're never going to change. You've got to take some big, bold steps. Understand that it's not going to be perfect along the way, share those imperfect moments with your team, with the broader organization, so they understand it, and also celebrate the wins, because I think a lot of people, when they say they can't do it, it's either they don't know how or they're afraid to fail. And I think that this is going to be a bumpy road for everybody, but we've all got to just lean in, try it, and learn from each other. - Where, for you, was there the greatest skepticism in the organization? I'm sure there were pockets where people said, "There's no way that AI has anything to do with what I do. In fact, it's going to slow me down, you're going to get me off target. This is not the place; focus somewhere else." - So where was there skepticism? I think it's easy to say it was everywhere. And there's still pockets that exist. But I think an early use case that everybody was focusing on for AI was content creation, and I think a lot of the content creators were very concerned that it was going to take their jobs, that it was going to cause them to not be able to do things the right way. In fact, when we did our research, we found that 80% of the creatives inside the organization at IBM were spending their time on derivative assets. When I go talk to a creative and I say, "Hey, do you want to spend 80% of your time on derivative assets?" they're like, "No, never." So now that number is at 40% and dropping, now that we've given them AI. When we went and we did a takeover of the Sphere in Las Vegas for Adobe Summit a few years ago, we used AI to help on the ideation process, but we had planned that the ideation process would be 15, 16 days. It took two days. And the creatives who were involved with it said, "It gave us new ideas that we hadn't thought about before." - So Jonathan, we've known each other a long time. - Long time. - You're a bold guy, you throw the javelin far ahead. For those who are a little more tentative, what advice can you give them from what you've learned to go further faster? - So I think there are a few things. One, you've got to ask questions. It might be internal, it might be external to peers in similar roles. It might also be coming to people like BCG. You all have been instrumental in some of our changes. As we've looked at our revenue technology, or rev tech, you all have been instrumental in how do we change that and how do we modernize it and bring it into an AI world? So go ask people questions. Two is find someplace relatively small, low-hanging fruit to try. Say, "I'm going to go after this one thing," and if you want to, just go after it in one market or two markets, and run a test and say, "Does this work, does it not work?" But the fundamental, the biggest thing that I think people have to do, Russell, is they've got to make sure they're incorporating the change management and the education into the process. You can layer in the technology, but if you're not using the technology because people don't know how or they're uncomfortable with it, that's where you're going to have your biggest problem. - Thank you, Jonathan, really appreciate your making the time. - Thanks, Russell.
