## 스크립트

Hello, I'm Aaron Behman, Global Lead for Retail and E-commerce at AMD, part of the Server Business Unit. I'm here in Paris at NRF Europe, and I'm joined by Marc Del Vecchio, who heads up business development for edge and computer vision at Supermicro. Marc, great to see you, man. How are you? Yeah, thanks for having us out here. I really appreciate it. Absolutely. So maybe share with me some of the big trends that you're seeing here at NRF or just in general. Sure, absolutely. Well, you know, AI has been the buzzword for quite some time now. And, you know, retail tends to lag behind sometimes in technology, but now they're catching up. And there's a lot of great use cases for AI in retail, ranging from inventory control, loss prevention is a big one, supply chain optimization. AI is all over between front of house, back of house use cases for retail. And we're seeing a lot of that here at NRF Europe. Yeah, that's great. I think one of the challenges with AI is a lot of companies know they need to implement it, but they just don't know how to or what to implement. I mean, one of the things that we've been working on, obviously, has been identifying some really crucial independent software vendors in the space and then putting together some solutions. Let's talk a little bit about that. You know, retailers in the end, they want a total solution. Just a piece of hardware doesn't do it for them. They need the hardware. They need the AI acceleration. They need the software that runs on top of it. And they need a system integrator to make it all happen and actually put it into the stores and support it. So that whole solution is what retailers are looking for. And that's why we're here demonstrating here today with Wobot. We're showing them that combined solution that gives them the AI computer vision story for retail. And I think one of the great things about a solution like Wobot is that it's very accessible. It's a low-code solution. Absolutely. And it comes built targeting an existing Supermicro-server that's based off of an AMD EPYC 8004 series CPU. So I think that that's actually quite powerful. It's a configurable platform, like you mentioned. It has that low-code interface. So it's not pigeon-holed and just doing one thing for a retailer. It can handle multiple use cases. And then consolidating that onto one Supermicro-server powered by AMD EPYC is a great solution. You know, it's designed for the edge. It's not meant for the data center. So it can go in those wider temperature range environments, smaller spaces, dirtier environments, I'll call it. And they can handle those AI workloads at the edge where, you know, you really want to process computer vision workloads at the edge. You don't want to do that in the cloud. So it's really a great solution for retailers. Absolutely. Marc, it's been great catching up with you. Really enjoy working with you and look forward to our continued success. Absolutely. Again, thanks for having me here.
