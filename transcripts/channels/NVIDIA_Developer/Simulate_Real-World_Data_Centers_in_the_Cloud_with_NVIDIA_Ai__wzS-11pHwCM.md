## 스크립트

Welcome to the NVIDIA Air Essentials Series, where&nbsp; we learn how to become NVIDIA Air Masters. NVIDIA&nbsp;&nbsp; Air allows you to instantly spin up networks&nbsp; of switches and servers in the cloud without&nbsp;&nbsp; allocating any physical resources, create&nbsp; identical replicas of real-world Data Center&nbsp;&nbsp; deployments, and accelerate your time to AI&nbsp; through simulation, validation, and automation.&nbsp;&nbsp; To access NVIDIA Air, go to air.nvidia.com&nbsp; and log in with your business email. When&nbsp;&nbsp; you log in, you will see your list of available&nbsp; simulations in your account. Let's talk about&nbsp;&nbsp; two different ways to create new simulations. The first way is to use the Demo Marketplace.&nbsp;&nbsp; The marketplace offers many pre-built&nbsp; simulations, also called demos or labs,&nbsp;&nbsp; that you can start from. Click "Launch" to&nbsp; create a copy of the SIM in your account.&nbsp;&nbsp; It is completely yours to learn from, explore,&nbsp; destroy, and rebuild. Click "Documentation"&nbsp;&nbsp; to read all about the lab and how it works.&nbsp; This link leads you to the GitLab repository&nbsp;&nbsp; for the lab. Feel free to check it out further. Another way of creating new SIMs is by using the&nbsp;&nbsp; drag-and-drop editor built directly into Air.&nbsp; To get started from the simulations homepage,&nbsp;&nbsp; click the "Create Simulation" button. Give the SIM&nbsp; a name and choose "Blank Canvas" for the type. You&nbsp;&nbsp; can also assign it to live in an organization&nbsp; and enable a ZTP script. Then click "Create."&nbsp; You can drop servers and switches from the&nbsp; system palette on the right directly into&nbsp;&nbsp; the workspace. As you may have noticed, they are&nbsp; based on Spectrum Hardware models. This does not&nbsp;&nbsp; affect the SIM itself but rather preconfigures&nbsp; the amount of ports available on the Node.&nbsp;&nbsp; You don't need to use them all. Click on the Node&nbsp; to set its properties. Give the node a host name,&nbsp;&nbsp; choose an operating system, and set CPU,&nbsp; memory, and storage. You can also enable some&nbsp;&nbsp; more advanced options, such as secure boot. Directly connect nodes together using the&nbsp;&nbsp; connector's area. You can choose which port&nbsp; on the current node you'd like to connect with&nbsp;&nbsp; any other node, then pick the port of that&nbsp; node you want to connect with. This forms&nbsp;&nbsp; a direct connection between the two nodes. On the system palette, there is an option&nbsp;&nbsp; to toggle enable out-of-band. Toggling this&nbsp; setting enables the out-of-band management&nbsp;&nbsp; network that connects each node with each&nbsp; other via eth0. It also adds an out-of-band&nbsp;&nbsp; management switch and an out-of-band management&nbsp; server to your simulation. When you are happy&nbsp;&nbsp; with your topology, click "Workspace",&nbsp; "Start Simulation" to power on the SIM.&nbsp; Now that we've created our new SIM, let's&nbsp; explore and interact with it. Click on your&nbsp;&nbsp; SIM from the simulations homepage to do so.&nbsp; You'll see your topology first thing. Using&nbsp;&nbsp; these tabs, you can also view your nodes, their&nbsp; details, and any direct links that they have.&nbsp; Double-click on a node to open its console.&nbsp; This button shows you the credentials to&nbsp;&nbsp; log in. You can pop the console out&nbsp; in its own window with this button.&nbsp; Single-click on a node to view its node&nbsp; properties. You can rebuild and reset&nbsp;&nbsp; individual nodes under Advanced options and&nbsp; actions. To rebuild the entire simulation,&nbsp;&nbsp; click "Workspace", "Rebuild All Nodes." Sharing simulations is a common use case&nbsp;&nbsp; within Air. Go to "Workspace" and click "Manage&nbsp; Users." Add the email you wish to share the SIM&nbsp;&nbsp; with and whether or not they have read-only&nbsp; privileges. This means that they will only&nbsp;&nbsp; be able to open and view the simulation, but&nbsp; they can still open node consoles and make&nbsp;&nbsp; changes that way in the SIM itself, but their&nbsp; actions within NVIDIA Air will be restricted.&nbsp; Ready to get started? Head&nbsp; on over to NVIDIA Air today.
