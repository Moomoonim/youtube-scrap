## 스크립트

Swisscom commercial, but none of the people in it who look Swiss. This is Switzerland. Time and again you come across hateful and offensive comments on the internet. It’s a huge problem. 29% of Swiss youth say they have been bullied online. For the victims, this can have severe consequences. Many struggle with depression and anxiety. The line between cyberbullying and hate speech is blurred. Today we’re looking at how to recognize both forms and what can be done about them. In cyberbullying, someone is harassed over a long period in chats, comments sections, forums or emails. Victims usually know the perpetrators personally, as they belong to the surrounding community. That is, it is often a case of real life being carried over onto the internet. Hate speech is found above all on social media. It is defined as statements that insult and discriminate against groups based on certain characteristics. such as gender, sexual orientation, religion, or skin colour. Marginalized people get disproportionately more hate speech. These are above all people on the receiving end of racism, homophobia, transphobia, fatphobia, and ableism. Women also get more hate than men. But we can all experience it. And most importantly, we can all do something about it. By learning how to deal with such comments properly, we not only take a stand for respectful interaction on the internet, but also against hate on the web. I get hate comments too. I’ve decided to show you an example. Someone wrote this comment to me, for example. “Swisscom commercial but none of the people in it look Swiss. This is Switzerland.” I could respond to this e.g. as follows. This is a racist statement because it implies that as a POC, I cannot be Swiss. The best tool against hate speech and cyberbullying is counterspeech, i.e. challenging the statements. You can correct false facts, point out if a comment breaks the law or simply contradict and leave positive comments. In this way, you show solidarity with the victim and lay down boundaries. What’s clear is that hate speech is not an opinion and can even break the law. On social media, you can report such comments via the platform and block the account. If a comment breaks the law, Instagram, for example, has to remove it. On stophatespeech.ch you can find out whether a comment breaks the law. We will also show you how else you can respond to hate speech on the Swisscom Campus. You will find the link in the description. If you are a victim of cyberbullying or hate speech, you can follow this rule: Stop, block, tell. End the discussion if it becomes too much for you, block unpleasant people and confide in someone. Seek help and find more information on the internet. You’ll also find more links in the description. But let’s be honest. Sometimes this kind of nasty comment is written before you know it. That’s why it’s important that we work together for better interactions on the internet, treat each other with respect and support each other. Send this video to all your friends so we can counter these haters. Because love is stronger than hate.
