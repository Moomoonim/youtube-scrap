- Zoox has two vehicles we actually test in parallel. We have our purpose-built robotaxi and our L3 Hybrid Toyota Highlanders. We test with our L3 vehicle on public roads in places like San Francisco, Las Vegas, and Seattle while we test our robotaxi on private roads. - The L3 architecture is specifically designed to mimic that of the robotaxi. The goal of the L3 is to collect as much data as possible with the same architecture that will be on the robotaxi in order to decrease the amount of work from one platform to the next. So by validating on these Toyota Highlanders that we have on public roads, we are able to collect that data, validate to build up confidence that the system is operating as designed. - Perception uses L3 testing in order to really validate and improve their solutions and also in order to prioritize what are the problems we'll be working on. Of course, all of software does that. - I was in the vehicle when we had our first public roads drive. It was in the middle of the night in Menlo Park. The software operator is responsible for collecting data and maintaining consistent call outs. That's essentially calling out the intended behavior of the robot. - We drive in these different geofences because each location has its own different learnings.
