Alex, I wanted to start by asking you, how do brands make the most of first-party data and using it at scale? - You know, our clients are thinking of first-party data more than they ever have, specifically because a lot of the channels that they're advertising on today are sharing fewer and fewer signals of them. So first-party data is becoming increasingly important for them to really not only understand who their customers are, but to better message to them, to better speak to them on the right channel at the right time. - Ryan, given the importance of first-party data for marketers, there's a great opportunity to collaborate with partners using it, but there's also a fear about privacy. How do clean rooms combat that problem? - Yeah, yeah, so clean rooms, at their heart, are aiming to give dataset owners control over how their data is used. So with a clean room, you pick the partners you're going to work with, you set the rules for how that engagement is going to function, and then you set the specific rules on how your data can be used. With AWS Clean Rooms, your data is never leaving your control. It stays in your environment, under your control, and it allows aggregate insights to be generated from that data. The real key here is that the results of an analysis on first-party data can be less sensitive than the raw data. So clean rooms lets you collaborate without having to share that raw data. - There are obvious key concerns about privacy, and this is now a new solution towards it. For you, what are you hearing the most from brands? What is their big fear about how much they're able to channel first-party data? - I'd say our brands, they certainly want to know more about their customers, but they also don't want to trip any red flags where they're going to be, especially on channels, I'd say in social, where they don't want to make a mistake and then be, not banned from advertising, but be blocked from reaching certain customers through certain channels. So, you know, there's this heightened sensitivity, I would say, to being overly conservative in terms of how they can connect with their customers. We see a lot of this, I've seen clients specifically around new cookie rules and cookie consent. Many of our clients, a huge decrease in the actual number of prospective customers who are actually coming to a site, staying on a site, because of the cookie consent barrier that's been put up now. So privacy for them, incredibly important, but I think many clients have been overly conservative, not understanding the lengths to which they should be, I would think, asking for that privacy request. - Ryan, looking ahead, what will differentiate the companies that are able to make the most of first-party data and security systems like clean rooms that can enable them? What's the future? What would you be advising people? - Sure, one of the big things is partnerships. So clean rooms solve a security and a privacy problem, but they also open up new opportunities to be able to bring signals from the brand's first-party data, signals from the publisher, signals from partners. So being able to collaborate and bring together a whole new picture of the consumer and use that to power campaigns is a really exciting opportunity that clean rooms open up. The other big thing I'd highlight is being open to not reinventing the wheel. So clean rooms open up new signals, they give you new access to data, but they don't change the fundamentals.
