(computer mouse clicks) So fine tuning, leverage transfer learning to efficiently inject niche expertise into a foundation model. The process involves updating the model weight through training on domain specific data while keeping the overall network architecture unchanged. Unlike a full pre-training, which require a massive dataset and compute power, fine tuning is highly sample and compute efficient. You can fine tune a model with as little as hundreds of data. Fine tuning isn't meant to add new information to your model. It's meant to change how your model behaves. Typical use case for fine tuning include ensuring the model responds in a specific way with a specific tone, or learning to generate complex outputs like in tasks that currently require a chain of prompts. As an additional benefit, fine tuning might reduce the number of tokens you need, or the latency for the request you send to the model. The main difference between continued pre-training and fine tuning is that fine tuning requires annotated data. This means that the data must be in a very specific format like a question and answer.
