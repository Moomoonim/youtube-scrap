## 스크립트

- At AWS Reinvent 2023, Dr. Werner Vogels talked about the seven laws of a frugal architecture. In this video, we'll recap those simple laws for building cost aware, sustainable, and modern architectures. We'll also deep dive into one of these laws, laws six, which tells us that cost optimization is incremental, and we'll look at what that means. Let's dive in. Law one. Make cost a non-functional requirement. Non-functional requirements define how a system is supposed to behave rather than what it's supposed to do. This includes considerations such as security, accessibility, availability, and more. But have you ever considered cost? When you consider cost implications early on and very continuously, you design your system to balance features, time to market, and efficiency. You then focus on maintaining lean and efficient code. Law two. Build systems that align cost to business. How long your system lasts depends on how well its costs are aligned to the business model. Law three. Architecting is a series of trade-offs. So we know that in architecture every decision comes with a trade-off. So we've had this before. Everything fails all the time. We lose whole data centers. In a nutshell, stuff happens. Knowing this, we need to prepare to defend against failure by investing in resilience, but resilience has a potential of affecting performance. It's therefore important to find the right balance between your technical and business needs to find the sweet spot that aligns with your risk tolerance and budget. Law four. Unobserved systems lead to unknown costs. Without careful observation and measurement, the true costs of operating a system remain invisible. Imagine taking your utility meter and hiding it under the sink. Lack of visibility enables wasteful habits. Bring that meter back from under that sink and make it more visible. This can lead to a big shift in behaviors, keeping costs in the forefront, which encourages sustainable practices. Law five. Cost aware architectures implement cost controls. At the heart of frugal architecture is robust monitoring that's combined with the ability to optimize costs. Well-designed systems allow you to take action on opportunities for improvement. For this to work, decompose applications into building blocks that you can tune independently, your infrastructure, your databases, your languages should all be tuneable. Law Six. Cost optimization is incremental. The pursuit of cost efficiency is ongoing. Even after we deploy, we must continue to visit our systems to improve optimization. Now, let's take a look at a short video to show you what I mean. Let's look at the ability to upgrade your code. With Amazon Q Code Transformation, you can upgrade your existing Java 8 based application to Java 11. When you request a code transformation, it examines all the source code in your existing Java repository to see what it needs to upgrade, and then upgrades it one by one. Once the upgrade is done, you can open the Java source code and compare what's changed. In this case, you can see that the Base64 encoding function has changed. You can also see the details of other changes. Law seven. Unchallenged success leads to assumptions. As software teams, we often fell into the trap of assuming our current technologies, our architectures, or languages will always be the best choice, simply because they have worked in the past. This can create a false sense of security that discourages questioning the status quo, or exploring new options, which could be more efficient, more cost effective, or more scalable. How many times have we caught ourselves saying things like, "We're a Java shop," or, "we've always done it this way." So these were the seven simple laws for building cost aware, sustainable and modern architectures.
