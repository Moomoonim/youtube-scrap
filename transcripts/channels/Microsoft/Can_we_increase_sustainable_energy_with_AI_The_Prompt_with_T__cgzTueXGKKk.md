Hey everybody, I'm here in Redmond, Washington, and today I'm going to be having a conversation with Jonathan Koomey. Let's start with the basics. Computers use electricity. When you type in a search query or a prompt for an AI model, that query goes across the network to a big warehouse full of computers. A lot of the headlines really paint a terrifying picture in that, the more of those queries we’re sending, the more tasks we're using AI for, the more we’re just heating the planet up. These headlines convey a sense of alarm, but they're being driven, I think in part by a mistake that people make. Computers as a whole use on the order of 5% of our electricity, and that's including datacenters. That 5% allows us to use the other 95% of the electricity and all of the fuels a lot more efficiently. We're doubling efficiency every 2.6 years or so. And now how do we think about weighing the pros and cons? Is there is there a way to like, weigh out the possibilities? Because ultimately as a society, we have to reduce greenhouse gas emissions to zero if we're going to stabilize the climate.
