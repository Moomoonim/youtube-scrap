## 스크립트

I think I first wanted to actually study physics but it seemed computer science was the more practical thing to do. That just seemed something that suited me, you know? We started off with a VC-20, a Commodore VC-20, my brother and myself. We would copy programs from magazines and modify and just explore and try out what's possible. Being in research, the most important part is that you're actually curious, that you're interested and you're curious. I run a research lab for AMD in Europe. I'm heading a team driving innovation into AI. We were looking at acceleration of different types of applications that would run in cloud infrastructure. There was a paper coming out, XNOR-Net, which demonstrated that you can implement neural networks with binary rates. And we thought, “Hold on a second! That could be an excellent fit for FPGAs.” So then we said, okay, we got to try this. And the results were really as great as we expected, and then we evolved from there. I'm really excited to put the AMD research lab on the map in Europe. We, as AMD, have this unique opportunity that we have all the compute fabrics that exist. It gives me an amazing playground to explore the realm of possibilities. I think there's a lot of great discoveries that will come from AI. Cancer cures, solving the energy crisis, new forms of antibiotics. There is a huge scope of what we can improve and I would love to participate in the innovation that leads to this.
